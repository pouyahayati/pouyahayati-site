SEO Pricing Models Compared: Retainer, Phased or Performance-Based

What each SEO pricing model really buys you, and which one to pick.

Quick summary

  • Most SEO providers charge a monthly retainer. Project fees come second, hourly third.
  • Phased is my label: a fixed-price first phase (audit and fixes), then a smaller monthly retainer with review dates.
  • Pay only if you rank is the weakest option. The only version that holds up is a base fee plus a bonus on business results.
  • All numbers here come from providers, not buyers. No independent buyer survey exists.
  • Best default for a small business: phased start, then retainer. Add a bonus only if your tracking is clean.
01The problem

Three proposals, three prices

For example, you asked three SEO providers for a quote. One wants $900 a month. One wants $3,500 for a "foundation project". One says "you only pay when you rank".

They are not selling the same thing. The first sells time. The second sells a defined piece of work. The third sells a promise about Google, which nobody controls. Google says it plainly: no one can guarantee a #1 ranking (Google, Do you need an SEO?).

So ask: what am I paying for, and what happens if it does not work?

02Method

How I researched this

I read the three large pricing surveys, Clutch's pricing data, Google's guidance and about a dozen agency pricing pages. I also asked five AI assistants the same question. Three limits:

  1. All numbers are provider-reported. Ahrefs, SE Ranking and Backlinko asked SEO providers, and all three sell SEO software. Clutch earns fees from some listings.
  2. No buyer-side data. I found no independent survey of what small businesses pay.
  3. The anti-performance articles come from firms that sell retainers.

Read the numbers as what providers say, not proof of what works.

03Data

What the numbers say

SE Ranking, 260 agencies: 53% prefer a retainer as their main model. Projects most often cost $500 to $2,000, hourly work $50 to $100. About 6% use performance based pricing, together with other models. The two surveys ask different questions, so do not compare their numbers directly. Sources: SE Ranking, SE Ranking on pay for performance.

What retainers cost

  • Ahrefs: the most common band is $501 to $1,000 a month (20.4%). Local SEO averages about $1,557.
  • SE Ranking: 64% of agencies charge under $1,000 a month.
  • Clutch: $3,199 a month on average, but that mix includes large accounts (Clutch pricing guide). Clutch does not list performance based as a model.

Ahrefs' averages assume the top of each price band, so they overstate. Use the bands, not the averages.

04Models

The three models in plain words

SEO retainer (monthly)

SEO is not a one time job, so a monthly retainer is the standard. A retainer with no named monthly deliverables is the easiest place to lose money.

Phased (project first, then retainer)

Phase 1 is usually a few weeks: audit, keyword plan, technical fixes, a roadmap. Phase 2 is a smaller retainer with a review at about 90 and 180 days. Write the scope down. This is my label, not an industry standard, but The Admin Bar, a newsletter for agency owners, describes the same "project first, then retainer" pattern, and all five AI assistants did too. I found no verified price data for phase 1, so ask for a written scope and a fixed price.

Example (illustrative only)

The Admin Bar, a newsletter for agency owners, gives this example of a phased quote: £5,000 upfront for the first project (audit, strategy and technical fixes), then £750 a month for ongoing work. It is one agency's illustration, not a market price. Use it to see the shape of the offer: a clear first scope, then a smaller monthly fee.

Performance based SEO (pay for performance)

With performance based SEO, you pay when an agreed result happens: a keyword reaches a position, traffic reaches a number, or you get a lead or a sale. Providers warn against it because:

  • The provider can pick easy keywords with almost no searches, hit the target, and your phone stays quiet.
  • A ranking target can reward short term tricks that cause penalties later.
  • The result depends on things the provider may not control: your developers, your content, your hosting.
  • Attribution is hard. Who gets credit for a lead?

SE Ranking, WebFX and Portent all argue against it. All three sell retainers, so weigh that.

The only version that holds up: a base fee you would accept anyway, plus a bonus tied to business results, not rankings. Ask for the formula in writing.

SEO guarantees: what is legitimate

Ahrefs says the only legitimate guarantee is lawyer drafted: the provider works for free if agreed results are not delivered, it covers long term performance and it excludes vanity metrics (Ahrefs). Don't sign a long contract on the strength of a short term guarantee.

05AI check

What AI assistants say

I asked ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Perplexity and Claude the same question, each in a fresh chat. All five said to avoid pure performance pricing, to use a base fee plus a bonus on leads or revenue instead, and to go project first, then retainer. Three put the phased project first (Gemini, Claude, Perplexity). Two called the retainer best overall, with a phased start (ChatGPT, Grok). One caution: my question used the words "phased project", and this is not independent evidence. It only shows where the common view has landed.

06Decide

How to choose: four questions

  1. Do you know what is wrong with the site? No: start phased. Yes, and it is a single fix: a project fee.
  2. Do you have someone to implement the work? If not, the retainer should include implementation, or you pay for advice that never ships.
  3. Is your tracking clean? Can you see leads and sales from organic search? Only then discuss a bonus.
  4. Can you walk away? Check the exit before the price.

07Contract

SEO contract checklist

Typical terms from agency and law firm guides. Guides, not rules, and not research.

  • Minimum term: 3 to 6 months is common, 12 on larger retainers. Avoid 12 months with no break clause.
  • Notice: 30 to 60 days. Watch auto renewal that locks you in once a year.
  • Ownership: you own the content, links and accounts (Search Console, Analytics, CMS). The provider keeps its tools.
  • Access: read only Search Console access during an audit, as Google advises.
  • Reporting: a baseline before work starts, a monthly report, and reviews at 90 and 180 days.
  • No verbal promises: if a salesperson promised rankings, get it in the contract. An "entire agreement" clause can erase what was said out loud.
  • Late payment: check whether the provider can suspend work or keep deliverables.

One real example (a 2013 forum thread, not a statistic): a buyer paid for a six month package after a phone sale, was promised better rankings out loud, found "no guarantee" in the contract, and could not cancel.

08FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How much does SEO cost for a small business?

The most common retainer band is $501 to $1,000 a month (Ahrefs, 439 providers), and 64% of agencies charge under $1,000 a month (SE Ranking, 260 agencies). See "What retainers cost" above for the Clutch average and why it runs higher.

Is performance based SEO worth it?

Pure "pay only if you rank" deals are the weakest option, because the provider can hit the target with easy keywords while your phone stays quiet. The only version that holds up is a base fee you would accept anyway plus a bonus on business results. Details are in the performance based SEO section above.

Can an SEO company guarantee rankings?

No. Google says no one can guarantee a #1 ranking. Ahrefs considers only a lawyer drafted guarantee legitimate. Details are in the SEO guarantees section above.
