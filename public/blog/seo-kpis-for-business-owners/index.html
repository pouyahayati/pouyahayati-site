Flat ≠ failed
01

One flat line, three stories.

You hired an SEO person or agency. There were targets. The date came, and the numbers didn't.

A deadline can't answer that. Even Google says some changes take effect in hours and others take months.

The same flat line can hide three very different stories. Click through them below.

Each story needs a different decision: push for execution, give it more time, or change the strategy. Firing a good SEO in story two is as costly as renewing a weak one in story three.

Next: what the experts measure.

02

What 8 experts agree on.

Instead of another list of 30 SEO metrics, I checked which ones the experts actually agree on.

I compared Google's guidance for site owners with the KPI guides from Ahrefs, Semrush, Search Engine Land, Search Engine Journal, Seer Interactive, Shopify and HubSpot. Click a bar to see who recommends it.

Only Google writes for you, the buyer. Every other guide is written for SEOs reporting to their boss. That's the gap this guide fills.

Put them in the order they happen, and you get a chain.

03

SEO is a chain.

Break one link and everything after it stops. Work has to go live before Google can show it, and people have to click before they buy.

Click a link to break it and see what happens next.

Semrush splits KPIs the same way. Links 1 and 2 are early signals: they move first, so you can fix problems while there's still time. Link 3 is the final result: it confirms the work, but it always shows up last.

That's also why a missed deadline is so misleading. It only looks at link 3, the slowest one.

Link 1 · Was the work done?

Work delivered and verified

The share of your SEO's recommendations that are actually live on your site. Sounds basic. It's also the metric almost nobody checks.

Read more

Here's why it matters: a lot of SEO work never ships. In a 2024 survey of technical SEOs, the most common answer was that only 40 to 60% of their recommendations got implemented. Often the blocker isn't the SEO. It's a developer with no time, or an approval stuck in someone's inbox, sometimes yours.

See an example

Where
Ask for a dated change log. Spot-check a few items on the live site.
Good sign
Key items live; blocked items say why.
Red flag
No log. Just "we did a lot of work."
Ask
"What did you recommend, what's live, and what's blocking the rest?"

Link 1 · Was the work done?

Indexing of your key pages

If Google hasn't indexed a page, it can't appear in search. So before you judge rankings or traffic, make sure your service, product and location pages are actually in.

Read more

Google recommends checking Search Console about once a month, including the indexing report and Manual Actions. Skip the site-wide page count; Ahrefs says that mostly matters for big sites. What matters are the pages that sell.

Sources: Google, Get started with Search Console; Google, Debugging drops in Search traffic; Ahrefs, SEO KPIs.

Where
Search Console → Indexing → Pages
Good sign
All key pages indexed. New ones in within weeks.
Red flag
Pages that sell "not indexed" for months, with no plan.
Ask
"Are all our key pages indexed? What's the plan for the rest?"
Does your SEO report show this?
Link 2 · Did visibility grow?

Non-brand clicks, versus last year

Clicks from searches that don't include your business name. People who type your name already know you. Non-brand clicks are new customers who found you because of SEO. That's the whole point.

Read more

Always compare with the same months last year, not last month, so seasons don't fool you. And use clicks, not impressions: Google confirmed impressions were misreported from May 2025 to April 2026. Clicks were fine.

Where
Search Console → Performance → Branded filter: non-brand

Good sign
Up versus last year, or steady while your market shrank.
Red flag
Only total traffic, and the growth is all brand.
Ask
"How did non-brand clicks change versus last year?"

Link 2 · Did visibility grow?

Search breadth

How many different non-brand searches send you at least one click. When it grows, Google is showing you for more of what your customers look for. It usually moves before traffic does, which makes it your best early signal.

Read more

Ahrefs puts search visibility at the top of its KPI list. The catch: SEO tools measure it with keyword lists your SEO picks. Counting real searches in Search Console uses Google's own data. It's free, and nobody can pick the keywords to make it look good.

Sources: Ahrefs, Semrush, Search Engine Journal, Seer, Shopify KPI guides.

Where
Search Console → Performance → Queries, non-brand → Export & count
Good sign
More searches over time, matching what you sell.
Red flag
Growth only in searches unrelated to your business.
Ask
"Which new searches are we showing up for?"
Does your SEO report show this?
Link 2 · Did visibility grow?

Where you rank

Don't rely on the single "average position" number. Count how many non-brand searches sit in positions 1 to 3, 4 to 10 and 11 to 20. Now you can see searches climbing toward the top.

Read more

Average position hides all this. It can even "improve" while you lose visibility: when low-ranking searches drop off, the average goes up. Google advises against focusing on absolute position, and Ahrefs calls single keyword rankings an inferior metric.

Searches that bring buyers climbing into the top 10 and top 3.
Red flag

Ask

Link 2 · Did visibility grow?

Clicks to pages that sell

Traffic isn't revenue. This one only counts non-brand clicks landing on pages that can make money: services, products, categories, locations, pricing.

Read more

Search Engine Land lists "pages generating traffic" as a real-world KPI. Eventbrite judged its own SEO by traffic to event listings, the pages that sell tickets. And HubSpot is the cautionary tale: its blog traffic fell sharply, yet its CEO said in 2025 that only about 10% of leads came from the blog. Popular posts don't always bring customers.

Sources: Search Engine Land, SEO KPIs; HubSpot, blogging KPIs; Google case study, Eventbrite; HubSpot Q2 2025 earnings call.

Where
Search Console → Performance → Pages, non-brand → Group by type
Good sign
Pages that sell gaining non-brand clicks.
Red flag
All growth on blog posts nobody buys from.
Ask
"How much search traffic lands on pages that sell?"
Does your SEO report show this?
Link 3 · Did it hit the business?

Verified leads and sales

Calls, forms, bookings and sales from organic search, confirmed in your own records, and compared with what you pay. That’s your SEO ROI. The one metric all eight sources agree on.

Read more

The key word is "confirmed". Some "conversions" are just page views or button clicks, and Google notes that Search Console and Analytics count visits differently. Your CRM settles it. Because this link moves last, don't use it as your only test in the early months. But in the end, it's the one that counts.

Sources: Ahrefs, Semrush, Search Engine Land, Search Engine Journal, Seer, Shopify, HubSpot; Google, Using Search Console and Analytics data; GA4 Help, key events.

Where
GA4 → Traffic acquisition → Organic Search → Key events
Then
Match against your CRM, call log or sales records.
Good sign
Organic leads rising, and sales confirms they're real.
Red flag
No key events, or leads your sales team calls junk.
Ask
"How many real leads came from organic, and how do you know?"
Does your SEO report show this?
Link 3 · For local businesses

Google Business Profile actions

Serve a local area? Then plenty of your customers never touch your website. They call or tap "directions" straight from your Google Maps listing. Your profile tracks every one of those actions. Semrush and Shopify both list them as SEO KPIs.

Sources: Google Business Profile Help, performance; Semrush and Shopify KPI guides.

Where
Your Business Profile → Performance
Good sign
Calls, directions and website clicks up versus last year.
Red flag
Never reported, or only "views".
Ask
"How many calls and direction requests came from our profile?"
Does your SEO report show this?

Next: what real companies measured.

06

What real companies measure.

12 businesses, from telecom giants to local trades. What matters is which metrics they trusted.

Not all evidence is equal: only three were proper controlled tests. Use the filter to compare.

Put all twelve side by side and four lessons jump out:

  1. Many SEO changes do nothing. Some hurt.

    A Pinterest title test had zero measurable effect. Some Etsy title tests hurt traffic badly. A change log is how you catch the bad ones fast.

  2. The pros measure money, not rankings.

    ZipRecruiter tracked non-branded traffic and conversions. Vodafone tracked leads and sales. Eventbrite tracked the pages that sell tickets.

  3. Traffic isn't the business.

    HubSpot's blog lost most of its traffic, yet it brought in only about a tenth of the company's leads.

  4. Some drops aren't your SEO's fault.

    HouseFresh took about two years to recover from a Google update. Check whether your whole market dropped before you blame anyone.
