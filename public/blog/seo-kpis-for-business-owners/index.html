Quick summary

Judge SEO by evidence, not deadlines.

Google says some SEO changes show up in hours and others take months, so a fixed deadline can't tell you if the work is good. Check three things in order: was the work done, did visibility grow, and did it bring real leads. This guide covers the 7 metrics to check, using free Google tools.

Flat ≠ failed
01The problem

One flat line, three stories.

You hired an SEO. The deadline came, and the numbers didn't.

A deadline can't tell you why. Google itself says some changes take hours and others take months. A flat traffic line usually means one of three things:

  1. The work never shipped. It's stuck with a developer or waiting for approval.
  2. It shipped and needs time. Early signals are moving; sales will follow later.
  3. It shipped and was wrong. Live for months, nothing moved. The strategy failed.

Each needs a different decision: push, wait or change. The metrics below tell you which one you're in.

Source: Google, SEO Starter Guide.

02The research

What 8 sources agree on.

I compared Google's guidance with the KPI guides from Ahrefs, Semrush, Search Engine Land, Search Engine Journal, Seer, Shopify and HubSpot.

Only one metric gets 8 out of 8: verified conversions from organic search. That's the final test. The others move first and show why conversions are or aren't coming, which is why you still need them.

03The framework

SEO is a chain.

Work goes live, Google shows it, people click, people buy. Break one link and everything after it stops.

Links 1 and 2 move first, so you can fix problems early. Link 3 moves last. A deadline only looks at Link 3, which is why it misleads.

Source: Semrush, SEO KPIs.

04The metrics

The 7 metrics (+1 for local).

All free, all in Google's own tools. You don't need to pull them yourself: send your SEO the free report template and ask for it every month.

01 · Link 1

Work delivered

How many of your SEO's recommendations are actually live. In a 2024 survey, most technical SEOs said only 40–60% of their recommendations ever get implemented.

Where
A dated change log. Spot-check a few items on your site.
Good sign
Key items live; blocked items say why.
Red flag
No log, just "we did a lot of work."
Ask
"What's live, and what's blocking the rest?"

Sources: Google, Do you need an SEO?; SEOFOMO, State of Technical SEO 2024.

02 · Link 1

Indexing of key pages

A page Google hasn't indexed can't rank. Check your service, product and location pages first; the site-wide total matters less.

Where
Search Console → Indexing → Pages
Good sign
All key pages indexed; new ones within weeks.
Red flag
Pages that sell "not indexed" for months.
Ask
"Are all our key pages indexed?"

Sources: Google, Get started with Search Console; Ahrefs, SEO KPIs.

03 · Link 2

Non-brand clicks over time

Clicks from searches without your business name: new customers SEO brought in. Compare with the same month last year if you have the data, so seasons don't fool you; otherwise watch the trend month by month. Use clicks, not impressions.

Where
Search Console → Performance → Branded filter: non-brand
Good sign
A steady rise over time.
Red flag
Only total traffic, and the growth is all brand.
Ask
"How have non-brand clicks changed over time?"

Sources: Ahrefs, Semrush, Search Engine Land, Shopify; Google, branded queries filter and data anomalies.

04 · Link 2

Search breadth

How many different non-brand searches bring you clicks. It grows before traffic does, so it's your best early signal, and it uses Google's data, not keywords your SEO picked.

Where
Search Console → Queries, non-brand → count
Good sign
More searches, matching what you sell.
Red flag
Growth only in irrelevant searches.
Ask
"Which new searches are we showing up for?"

Sources: Ahrefs, Semrush, Search Engine Journal, Seer, Shopify.

05 · Link 2

Where you rank

Skip "average position"; it can rise while you lose visibility. Count your non-brand searches in positions 1–3, 4–10 and 11–20 instead, and watch them climb.

Where
Search Console → Queries + Position
Good sign
Buyer searches moving into the top 10 and top 3.
Red flag
Only average position or hand-picked screenshots.
Ask
"How many important searches reached the top 3?"

Sources: Google, Debugging drops in Search traffic; Ahrefs and Search Engine Land, SEO KPIs.

06 · Link 2

Clicks to pages that sell

Traffic isn't revenue. Count only non-brand clicks to pages that can make money: services, products, locations, pricing.

Where
Search Console → Pages, non-brand
Good sign
Pages that sell gaining clicks.
Red flag
All growth on blog posts nobody buys from.
Ask
"How much traffic lands on pages that sell?"

Sources: Search Engine Land, SEO KPIs; HubSpot, blogging KPIs; Google case study, Eventbrite.

07 · Link 3

Verified conversions

Calls, forms, bookings and sales from organic search, confirmed in your CRM and compared with what you pay. That's your SEO ROI, and the only metric all 8 sources agree on. It moves last, so don't judge on it alone in the first months.

Where
GA4 → Organic Search → Key events, checked against your CRM
Good sign
Organic leads rising, and sales confirms they're real.
Red flag
"Conversions" that are just page views or clicks.
Ask
"How many real leads came from organic, and how do you know?"

Sources: all 8 KPI guides; Google, Search Console and Analytics data; GA4 Help, key events.

+1 · Local businesses

Business Profile actions

Local customers often call or get directions straight from Google Maps, without visiting your site. Your profile counts those actions.

Where
Business Profile → Performance
Good sign
Calls and directions rising over time.
Red flag
Never reported, or only "views".
Ask
"How many calls came from our profile?"

Sources: Google Business Profile Help; Semrush and Shopify KPI guides.

05The traps

Numbers that fool you.

They look good in a report and prove very little.

AI search? Worth watching, but the data is still limited. Don't judge your SEO on it yet.

Sources: Google, Ahrefs, Shopify, Semrush.

06The proof

What real companies measure.

11 businesses, from telecom giants to local trades. Three were controlled tests, three Google case studies, five agency or self-reported.

Three lessons:

  1. Many changes do nothing. Some hurt.

    Pinterest saw zero effect from a title test; some Etsy tests cut traffic. A change log catches this fast.

  2. The pros measure money, not rankings.

    ZipRecruiter, Vodafone and Eventbrite tracked conversions, leads and pages that sell.

  3. Some drops aren't your SEO's fault.

    HouseFresh took two years to recover from a Google update. Check your whole market first.
